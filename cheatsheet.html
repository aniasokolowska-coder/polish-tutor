Gender, agreement & useful patterns

  • Nouns have grammatical gender: masculine, feminine, or neuter. Adjectives and past-tense verbs agree with gender and number.
  • Past singular: robiłem (male speaker), robiłam (female speaker); plural: robiliśmy (masculine-personal group), robiłyśmy (non-masculine-personal group).
  • Adjective agreement examples: dobry film (masculine), dobra książka (feminine), dobre dziecko (neuter).
  • Negation commonly changes a direct object to the genitive: Mam czas → Nie mam czasu (I have time → I don’t have time).
  • Polish has no articles. Context and word order tell you whether a noun means “a/the.”
  • Verbs have aspect: imperfective describes process, repetition, or background; perfective presents a bounded or completed event. Many perfective verbs have no present-time meaning; their present forms refer to the future.
  • Word order is flexible, but endings carry grammatical roles. Pronouns are often omitted when the verb already identifies the subject.

Pronunciation reminders

ą — nasal vowel; pronunciation shifts by context.
ę — nasal vowel, often less nasal at word end.
ł — like English “w” in most modern speech.
sz / cz / rz — roughly “sh” / “ch” / voiced “zh”.
